3. Gardening and safety

Gardening involves physical activity, tools, soil, water, chemicals and living things. It also involves your building. Please use common sense and take appropriate precautions.

General information, not professional advice

Our content is general educational information. It is not professional horticultural, landscaping, structural engineering, medical, veterinary, legal or pest-control advice. Plant care depends on your local climate, light, water, soil, building and the particular plant in front of you. What worked for us may not work for you.

Your space and your building

  • Check your building’s rules and any lease or society restrictions before installing planters, hanging baskets, trellises or irrigation.
  • Do not exceed balcony, railing or roof load limits. Do not hang weight over an edge or public space without professional advice.
  • Secure pots against wind, keep walkways clear, and make sure water drains away from walls, wiring and neighbouring property.
  • If you are unsure about structural safety, waterproofing or electrical work, consult a qualified professional.

Plants, pets and people

Some plants are toxic or irritating to humans or animals, and some are invasive in certain regions. Verify before bringing a plant into a home with children or pets, and before planting anything outdoors. Never eat a plant unless you are certain of its identity and that it has not been treated with unsafe chemicals.

Tools, chemicals and water

Use tools and equipment safely and keep them away from children. If you use fertilizer, pesticide or other chemical products, follow the manufacturer’s label and all local laws, and keep them away from waterways, pets and children. Rainwater, greywater and stored water can carry contaminants; use appropriate judgment and local guidance.

You are responsible for your own actions and for the safety of your garden, your building and the people around you.
